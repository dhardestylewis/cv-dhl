## Applying for the Anthropic Fellows Program and how I created an AI Red Team to help me do it

I saw an opportunity. I ignored it. And then I went for it.

Alyssa Fu Ward, PhD

Jul 01, 2026

AI this, AI that, promotion, new role, happy news, Anthropic is paying talented people $3,850 per week to spend 4 months doing frontier AI research.

I stopped my scroll on LinkedIn. *Come again?*

It was a post calling for applications about an Anthropic Fellows Program focused on AI Safety.

Screenshot of the LinkedIn repost

I read through the post, thought, “How neat,” and moved on. Part of me felt pulled to go for it, but the focus area was on AI Safety. It was too serious of a topic, too high stakes for me. I wasn’t sure what I could contribute.

---

But the post kept popping up in my random thoughts ticker.

*Four months paid research in the Bay Area, and the opportunity to work closely with one of the top AI companies in the world.*

And I was a recent Claude convert, ushered in through months of daily use of Claude Code.

The deadline to apply was Sunday, April 26, 2026. It was Friday, April 24 when I decided to go for it.

Here’s the story of how I used AI as a partner, reviewer, and support robot to do it.

## Phase 1: Finding my footing

The first thing I did was check in with Claude to help me feel my way through this space.

### Step 1.1: What you think, Claude?

I shared the Anthropic Fellows application link to Claude and asked it, *“Can you see this?”*

I don’t tend to write long well-thought prompts. I like to discover and co-create the conversation with the AI. I’ve found this helps me manage its responses, as well as my own.

Screenshots of Anthropic Fellows Application + Claude chat

Claude confirmed it could see the link and shared a summary, including the workstreams to apply for.

Given my background in behavioral science, AI Safety and Economics were the clear choices for me with a leaning toward Economics.

### Step 1.2: Here’s my resume

Next, I told Claude:

> “I think I’ll apply tomorrow…. This is the resume I just put together for [a different AI-type role] so it’s AI oriented”

Claude started its response with a reassuring, “*Oh interesting! The deadline is April 26  -  that’s tight but doable.*”

It asked me which workstream I was interested in and proposed a “lean plan” to fit into two days. All very good and helpful, thank you AI.

### Step 1.3: Whoops, I missed that

Then, the AI caught something I missed.

When I looked at the application, I only saw it ask for a resume. I thought, *Okay Anthropic, vet people based just on their resumes, cool cool. Your AI reviewers must be mighty fine.*

But then, Claude referenced spending time on answering questions, and I was confused.

“It just says submit application? With resume?” I told it.

But Claude insisted there was another application.

And sure enough, Claude was right.

There was a time where I used to pore over these applications, searing every word into my perfectionistic anxious brain.

Now, I’m so go-with-the-flow that I do miss these kinds of details.

Am I giving up my critical thinking by letting AI catch the details for me? Maybe, but in this case, I don’t mind.

## Phase 2: Spawning an AI Red Team

As I reviewed the questions, an idea shot through me. I had just heard of a colleague who used a Red Team to guide him as he wrote an application for a consulting gig.

I was almost giddy thinking about how this was a perfect opportunity to try one out for me too.

So I asked Claude, with fast talking-to-an-AI and missing words included:

> “Can you create for me red team personas and what should they be and how would store them and how would I use them”

Some people may have been super specific in how to build the Red Team.

My style as I said is pretty loose. I didn’t know what I wanted, and I didn’t feel a strong need to figure it out first. I like to see what the AI will do, then mold from there.

In fact, Claude tried asking clarifying questions, but I just told it, “I don’t know, just curious what it looks like.”

So Claude created 4 personas with different critical lenses:

* **The ML Researcher**  -  “What are her empirical research chops?”
* **The Economist**  -  “Her behavioral science is social psych, not econ. Does she understand causal inference, labor markets?”
* **The Safety Purist**  -  “Is she actually motivated by AI risk or just career-opportunistic?”
* **The Insider**  -  “With 500 applicants with big tech backgrounds, how is she differentiated?”

Then, Claude suggested all on its accord creating a quick interactive artifact where I could paste in my answer, pick a persona, and the persona pushes back.

That sounded great to me. Off Claude went to beep boop its way into a Red Team.

## Phase 3: Figuring out what to write

After building the Red Team, I didn’t actually use it right away.

Instead, I had a long conversation with Claude about the space we were working in first  -  Economics.

### Step 3.0: Economics what?

Here’s where I have to share some background and confessions.

I took an Economics class in college, and I was bored out of my mind. Markets this, expected value that. Bleugh. Give me a brain or a controlled lab study that tells a story about humans, and I’m all in.

Then, when I went to grad school to study Social Psychology, it turns out there are actual discipline-based tensions between the two fields.

I won’t go into all of it. But regardless of the discipline differences, it was hard to ignore that Economics seemed overrun by elitist white dudes, whereas Social Psychology was diverse, nuanced, and human-centered, especially in my area, which was focused on culture, race, and ethnicity.

This is all to say that preparing an application focused on Economics felt like a stretch for me. But I was willing to try.

### Step 3.1: Tell me more, o knowledgeable AI

I asked Claude to share with me research papers in Economics that would be good for me to know, really get myself into the research mindset.

And Claude shared with me a list of impressive individuals (all men though, this drives me crazy) and concepts.

Claude's response on Economics and AI Safety Research Areas

What’s nice was  - Claude volunteered “*Where YOUR angle lives*” and how my most interesting research question sits at the intersection of “*How do humans actually behave when they adopt AI tools and what does that mean for outcomes?”* I appreciated that it helped me capture what was important to me while also framing it for Economics.

### Step 3.2: Tell me what you know about me

Claude asked me where I wanted to dive in deeper.

I responded by asking what it knew about me.

I like to do this to see what it’s captured about me, how it’s read between the lines of what I’ve told it. I also like to do it when I’m tired and my brain is mush. My goal isn’t to take what it’s written and use it as my own. It’s to collect fodder, mulch, and seeds that I can squish and squeeze and turn into something written by me.

Claude shared that based on what it knew about me, my writing, and my human-AI frameworks, a few things stand out:

* **The human is the unit of analysis.** My framework isn’t about what AI can do; it’s about what humans can do with it.
* **Behavior change is the real outcome.** It’s not about adoption metrics or tool usage; it’s  -  did something actually change how a person thinks or works.
* **Accessibility and translation matter morally.** I write and teach the way I do because I believe people deserve to understand the tools reshaping their work.
* **The “human in anyone” idea.** My little orange character represents this  -  AI should bring out more humanity, not less.

I don’t think I read this response very carefully when I first read it. I was stressed at the time and wanted to move fast.

Reading it back now, I’m quite touched. And these notes captured what I believe beautifully.

### Step 3.3: Let’s talk research ideas

Then we turned to concrete research ideas.

We started big. I posed the question  - 

> What could the economy look like if we started with the premise of human possibility and imagination instead of productivity and automation?

Claude suggested ways to turn this into a testable question, like for example, *“How do worker outcomes differ when organizations adopt AI with a human development framing instead of cost reduction?”*

I used to feel panic in the safety space. When I hear big questions with potentially devastating consequences, my brain freezes and feels pressure to create solutions immediately. But it’s crazy to think my little ol’ one-person self can solve a massive society problem!

Working with the AI helps me turn the problem into something that feels like finding my socks and shoes to go outside. Take it one small piece at a time, preferably in a space that feels familiar and can extend what I already know.

My energy started to rise, and it got me excited to dig in.

And then I went to bed.
